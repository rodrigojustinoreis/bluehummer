Home remodeling in Potomac, MD.

Blue Hammer Remodeling is based in Olney and plans kitchens, primary baths and lower levels for homes in Potomac 20854, and in North Potomac and Darnestown. The rooms can be planned one at a time or as one connected project, with the layout, cabinetry and surfaces worked out before anything is ordered.

Kitchen, primary suite and lower level in Potomac

What each room can become depends on the space around it and on a few conditions of the property.

Kitchen remodeling

If there is space behind or beside the kitchen, a pantry or scullery takes the small appliances and a second sink off the main counters. If the kitchen opens to a family room, the island becomes the divider, and its seating side and working side are planned separately. If the range is a large one, the hood and its duct are sized to match.

Primary bathroom

If the bath and the closet share a wall, planning them together can free space for a larger shower. If you want a freestanding tub, its drain position and the floor under it are checked first. If you want a heated floor, it is laid before the tile is set.

Finished lower level

If there is a door to the yard, the daylight side takes the main room. A gym, a media room and a bar each need different wiring and ventilation, so the uses are fixed before framing. If the house is on septic and you want a guest bedroom downstairs, the County reviews the bedroom count first.

Well, septic and permits in Potomac

Some Potomac houses are on a well or a septic system, and some neighborhoods have an association with rules of its own.

  • County and WSSC

    Potomac is not one of Montgomery County’s municipalities, so the Department of Permitting Services issues the building permit. Plumbing and gas go through WSSC Water.

  • Well and septic

    If the house is on a septic system, the County evaluates added habitable space and added bedrooms against the capacity of the system. Required upgrades are finished before the building permit is approved.

  • Glen Hills

    The County reports that most existing homes in the Glen Hills study area use wells and septic systems.

  • Covenants and associations

    The County does not enforce private covenants. If the neighborhood has an association, its rules are checked with the association.

What do you need to know about the house before design starts?

The measured rooms, where the water, gas and electrical lines run, and the size of the electrical service. We also ask whether the house is on public sewer or a private septic system. Structural questions are settled by an on-site assessment.

Can a pantry or scullery be added to the kitchen?

If there is adjoining space to take it from, such as a closet, a back hall or part of the next room, yes. It needs its own lighting and outlets, and plumbing if it will have a sink. A measured drawing shows whether the kitchen keeps enough room once it is carved out.

Can the primary bathroom and closet be remodeled together?

Yes, and planning them as one project lets the wall between them move. That can make room for a larger shower, a separate toilet room or a longer vanity. Drain locations are checked first, because they limit where fixtures can go.

What can a finished lower level include?

A family room, a gym, a media room, a bar, a bathroom or a guest room, alone or in combination. Each use has its own wiring and ventilation needs, and some need plumbing. Moisture and ceiling height are checked before any of them is drawn.

What should we have ready for the consultation?

The project address, photos of the rooms, and a wish list sorted into essential and optional. Bring drawings from an architect or designer if you have them, and septic records if the house has a system. A budget range and your timing complete the picture.

Our house is on septic. Can we add a bedroom or a guest suite?

That is decided by the County’s review of the property. Under its guidelines, adding bedrooms calls for a septic permit record on file and an approved septic reserve area for the total number of bedrooms proposed. If there is no record on file, the County requires water table and percolation tests to establish a reserve area, and a new septic system, before it approves added bedrooms or habitable space. Any required upgrade or replacement is finished before the building permit is approved. The County also determines which rooms count as bedrooms.
