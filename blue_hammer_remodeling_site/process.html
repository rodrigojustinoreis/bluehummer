How projects run

Clarity before construction.

A remodel is easier to live through when you know what happens next. This is how a design-led project with Blue Hammer moves from a first conversation in your kitchen to the final walk-through.

Step by step

From first idea to finished room.

The sequence is the same whether it is a hall bath in Kensington or a whole lower level in Clarksville; only the duration of each step changes.

Realistic overall timelines

Hall bath: about 3–4 months from first call to finished room. Full kitchen: 5–7 months. Primary bath or finished basement: 5–8 months. Most of that is design, selections, ordering and permit review; construction itself is the shorter part.

A note on the unknowns

In 1920s–1990s homes we often find old wiring, cast-iron drains, undersized framing or moisture once walls are open. We price what we can see, carry a stated allowance for what we can reasonably expect and talk to you before spending any of it.

1

Conversation and walkthrough

You tell us what is not working and what you hope for. We visit the house, look at structure, mechanicals and access, and talk openly about a realistic budget range for the scope you have in mind.

2

Design and selections

Measured drawings, layout options and 3D views where they help. We visit cabinet, stone, tile and plumbing showrooms around Montgomery and Howard County with you, so every selection is made before anything is ordered.

3

Written scope and price

A line-by-line scope, the selections schedule, allowances that are clearly marked as allowances, the payment schedule and the construction calendar. You sign when it reads the way you expect it to be built.

4

Permits and ordering

We file with Montgomery County DPS, the City of Rockville or Howard County DILP, plus any town or historic-district review. Cabinetry, stone, tile and fixtures are ordered and checked on arrival, so construction starts with everything on hand.

5

Construction

Dust barriers and floor protection go up first. Demolition, rough-in, inspections, drywall, tile, cabinetry, counters, finish plumbing and electrical, paint and trim, in that order, with a weekly update and a running decision log.

6

Walk-through and follow-up

We walk the finished room against the scope, complete the punch list and hand over care instructions and the product and warranty documentation for everything installed. Then we check in once you have lived with it.

More Frequently Asked Questions
Can we stay in the house during the remodel?

Usually, yes. We set up a temporary kitchen for kitchen projects and sequence bathroom work so one is always working. Whole-level projects are planned room by room with you.

How do you protect the rest of the house?

Sealed dust barriers with negative air where needed, hard floor protection on the path from the door, daily clean-up and a single point of entry agreed with you in advance.

What if I change my mind mid-project?

It happens. Changes are written up with their cost and schedule effect before the work is done, so the final invoice matches what you approved.

Who is on site day to day?

A lead carpenter runs the job day to day, alongside licensed plumbing and electrical trades. You will know everyone by name before they start.
