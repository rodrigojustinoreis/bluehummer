Our process, step by step.

A remodel is easier to live through when you know what happens next. These are the six steps a project with Blue Hammer follows, from the first conversation about your home to the final walk-through, and what is decided at each one.

The six steps of a project

The order is the same for a hall bathroom, a kitchen or a whole lower level. What changes is how much there is to decide at each step.

  1. Conversation and walkthrough

    You tell us what is not working and what you hope for. We visit the house, look at the structure, the mechanical systems and the access, and talk through the budget factors for the scope you have in mind.

  2. Design and selections

    Measured drawings and layout options come first. Then the selections, from cabinetry to plumbing fixtures, so each one is settled before anything is ordered.

  3. Written scope and price

    A line-by-line scope, the selections list, allowances marked as allowances, the payment schedule and the construction schedule. You sign when it reads the way you expect the room to be built.

  4. Permits and ordering

    The permit office depends on the address: Montgomery County DPS for most County addresses, the City of Rockville inside city limits, Howard County DILP in Howard County. In Montgomery County, plumbing and gas permits go through WSSC Water, and some municipalities require their own approval. Which permits the project needs, and who applies for each, is written into the agreement. The start date then depends on permit approval and on delivery of the made-to-order items.

  5. Construction

    Demolition, rough-in, inspections, drywall, tile, cabinetry, counters, finish plumbing and electrical, then paint and trim. How dust, floors and access through the house are handled is agreed with you before the first day on site.

  6. Final walk-through

    We walk the finished room with you against the written scope, list anything still open and close it out.

What sets the schedule, and what the walls can hide

  • What sets the schedule

    Four things decide how long a project runs: how many design decisions are still open, how long the products you choose take to arrive (made-to-order cabinetry and stone take longer than stock items), the permit review for your address, and the amount of construction. Each one gets a date in the written schedule once the scope and selections are known.

  • What is behind the walls

    Wiring, drain lines, framing and signs of moisture are only partly visible before demolition. The walkthrough records what can be seen. The written scope says what is included, what is an allowance, and how a hidden condition is handled if one turns up: described in writing with its cost and schedule effect, and approved by you before that work is done.

Living in the house during the work

These are settled during planning, for your house and your household, and written down with the scope.

More remodeling questions
Can we stay in the house during the remodel?

It depends on the scope and on the house, and it is decided during planning. If the kitchen is being remodeled, the plan needs to cover where you will cook, wash dishes and keep the refrigerator while it is out of use. If the only bathroom in the house is being remodeled, the plan needs to cover the time it has no water, or whether being away for that stretch makes more sense. If there is a second bathroom, the plan says which one stays in use and when.

How is the rest of the house protected?

That is part of the plan for your house: where the work area is closed off, which door and path are used for the work, how floors on that path are protected, and how the site is left when work stops each evening. It is agreed with you before work starts.

What if I change my mind mid-project?

It happens. A change is written up with its cost and its effect on the schedule before the work is done, so the final invoice matches what you approved.

Who is on site day to day?

We confirm who will be on site, and when, with you before construction starts.
