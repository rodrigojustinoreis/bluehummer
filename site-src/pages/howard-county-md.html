What we remodel in Howard County

Each one comes down to a few decisions about layout and storage.

  • Kitchen remodeling

    If the layout already works, new cabinets, counters and lighting go in on the same plan. If the island is too small or in the way, its size is set by the clearances around it, then by seating. If the garage door opens into the kitchen, a bench, hooks and tall cabinets by that door keep bags and shoes off the counters.

  • Bathroom remodeling

    If several people share a hall bath, two sinks and a door between the vanity and the tub let two of them use it at once. If the primary bath has a tub nobody uses, that floor can go to a larger shower. If towels and supplies have no home, a linen cabinet is drawn in before the tile is chosen.

  • Finished lower level

    If the level has a door to the yard, the daylight side takes the family room. If you want a home office, it goes where a door can close off the noise. If you want a bedroom or a guest suite and the house is on a septic system, the Health Department reviews the floor plan.

Howard County has its own permit office

Building and trade permits come from one County department. Septic systems and the two local historic districts have reviews of their own.

  • Howard County DILP. The Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits issues building, plumbing, electrical and HVAC permits. Montgomery County’s rules and offices do not apply here.
  • Interior alterations. The County’s residential permit handout uses a kitchen remodel with a wall removed and a finished basement as its examples of an interior alteration. It asks for existing and proposed floor plans, and for engineer calculations if a load-bearing wall comes out.
  • Well and septic. The application records whether the house has public or private water and sewer. If it is not on public sewer, the Health Department reviews a floor plan to count the bedrooms the septic system has to serve.
  • Historic districts. In the Ellicott City and Lawyers Hill local historic districts, the Historic Preservation Commission reviews exterior alterations.

What do you look at in the house before design starts?

The measured rooms, where the plumbing and electrical lines run, and the size of the electrical service. We ask whether the house has public water and sewer or a well and septic system, because the County’s permit application asks the same. Structural questions are settled by an on-site assessment.

Can we keep the kitchen layout and replace everything else?

Yes. With the sink, range and walls staying where they are, the project is new cabinets, counters, backsplash, lighting and appliances. It is the most contained kind of kitchen remodel. Flooring is the open question: if the new cabinets do not sit on the same footprint as the old ones, the floor under them has to be dealt with.

How can one hall bathroom serve several people?

Divide it. A vanity with two sinks in the outer part and a door to the tub and toilet lets two people use the room at once. Whether that fits depends on the length of the room and where the drains are. Drawers and a linen cabinet matter as much as the fixtures.

Can the lower level have a bedroom or a guest suite?

That is decided in permit review. The County’s application records the bedrooms and bathrooms a project affects. If the house is not on public sewer, the Health Department reviews the floor plan to count bedrooms against the septic system. An on-site assessment comes first, and the permit office confirms what the room needs.

What should we have ready for the consultation?

The address, photos of the room, and a list of what is not working, sorted into essential and optional. Bring floor plans of the house if you have them. A budget range and the timing you have in mind complete the picture.

Who issues remodeling permits in Howard County?

Howard County’s Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits. It issues the building permit and the plumbing, electrical and HVAC permits. Montgomery County’s permit offices have no role here. Inside the Ellicott City and Lawyers Hill historic districts, the Historic Preservation Commission also reviews exterior alterations.
