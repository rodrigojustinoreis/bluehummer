From 1890s Victorians to 1950s Capes.

Each kind of house here has its own logic, and the remodel has to follow it.

  • Kensington historic district (Old Town). 1890s–1910s Victorians and Queen Annes with ten-foot ceilings, wraparound porches and kitchens tucked in rear ells. Owners want a kitchen that honors the house: beadboard, painted cabinetry, soapstone, a farmhouse sink under the bay window.
  • Garrett Park. The whole town is a historic district: Victorians along the railroad, 1920s “Chevy houses” built by Maddux, Marshall & Co., and a notable group of mid-century modern homes. Interiors are small by today’s standards, so planning is about editing, not adding.
  • Parkwood, Rock Creek Hills, Kensington Heights, Chevy Chase View, Homewood. 1940s–60s brick Capes, ramblers and colonials with closed kitchens, one-and-a-half baths and unfinished basements, good candidates for a kitchen opening, a second full bath and a finished lower level.

What homeowners here ask us for.

Small footprints, so most of the work is about using space better.

  • Open a Cape’s kitchen without losing its character

    Take down the wall to the dining room, keep the ceiling line and the original window, add a peninsula or a compact island and run painted cabinetry to the ceiling with inset doors and brass.

  • A second full bath

    Convert a half bath, a closet or a corner of an upstairs bedroom into a full bath with a glass shower, tile that suits the house and ventilation done properly through a slate or shingle roof.

  • Finish the basement for real living

    Family room, laundry, full bath and a guest room with egress, with moisture control, insulation and headroom assessed before the design is drawn.

Historic districts change the sequence.

Historic review time is planned into the schedule from the start.

  • Montgomery County DPS. Structural and electrical permits for Kensington, Garrett Park and the surrounding unincorporated neighborhoods. Plumbing and gas permits come from WSSC Water.
  • Historic Area Work Permits (HAWP). Exterior changes to homes in the Kensington and Garrett Park historic districts go before the County’s Historic Preservation Commission. Interior-only work does not need a HAWP, but a new window for a kitchen sink, a roof vent or an egress window might. We check early and prepare the application.
  • Town permits. The Town of Kensington and the Town of Garrett Park each have their own permit or notification requirements and rules on construction hours and parking. We coordinate them with the County permit.
  • Older-home conditions. Balloon framing, plaster and lath, original wiring and galvanized supply lines are common in pre-1940 homes here. We investigate during design so the written scope is realistic.

Does a kitchen remodel in the historic district need historic approval?

Not if the work is entirely interior. If the design adds or changes a window, door, vent or dormer, that exterior change needs a Historic Area Work Permit from the County’s Historic Preservation Commission, and we build the review time into the schedule.

Can you work with the small footprints in Garrett Park?

Yes. We measure to the quarter inch, use inset cabinetry and built-ins to reclaim space, and plan storage so a modest kitchen or bath works better than a larger, careless one.

Do you serve Parkwood, Rock Creek Hills and Chevy Chase View too?

Yes. The unincorporated neighborhoods around Kensington and Garrett Park are all part of the same service area and the same twenty-minute drive from Olney.

Can you add a full bath upstairs in a 1950s Cape?

Usually. The dormered second floor often has a closet or knee-wall space that can become a compact full bath; drain routing and roof ventilation are the details we resolve during design.
