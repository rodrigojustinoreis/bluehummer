What we check behind the tile

The first visit looks past the finishes. We check where the drain and supply lines run, how the floor is framed, where the fan vents and whether there are signs of an old leak. Those findings decide which fixtures can move and what the scope has to include.

What is decided before demolition

A bathroom is a small room with a lot inside its walls. These choices are made first.

  • Shower or tub

    A tub with a shower, a walk-in shower, or a separate tub. A curbless entry depends on how the floor is framed, which we look at on site.

  • Waterproofing

    The membrane behind the tile matters more than the tile. The system, and how it meets the drain, is written into the scope.

  • Vanity and storage

    Drawers, a recessed medicine cabinet and niches sized for what you use, so the counter stays clear.

  • Tile and surfaces

    Porcelain or natural stone, compared for grip on a wet floor and for how much upkeep each one asks.

  • Lighting and ventilation

    Vanity lights and mirrors are planned together to keep shadows off your face. The exhaust fan and its duct route go on the same drawing.

Plumbing and building permits for a bathroom

In much of our area the plumbing permit and the building permit come from different offices. The requirement for your address is confirmed in writing before work is accepted.

See all service areas
  • WSSC Water, in Montgomery County. WSSC requires most plumbing projects, interior re-piping included, to be done by a plumber who holds a WSSC Water license. Permit applications go through its online system.
  • County permits. The Montgomery County Department of Permitting Services lists electrical work and interior alterations among projects that will likely need a permit. Caulking, painting and new floor coverings are on its list of work that most likely will not.
  • City of Rockville. For a property in the City of Rockville, the City issues the trade permits: plumbing, electrical, mechanical.
  • Howard County. In Howard County, plumbing and electrical permits come from the County’s Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits, the same office that issues building permits.

Bathroom remodeling questions

More remodeling questions
How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Maryland?

It depends on what moves. A bathroom that keeps its layout costs less than one where the drains and supply lines are relocated. Tile quantity, shower glass, the vanity and the condition of the old pipes account for most of the rest. A written scope, based on your drawings and selections, turns those factors into a price.

Can you remodel a bathroom without moving the plumbing?

Yes. Keeping the tub, toilet and sink where they are is the simplest scope, and the room can still get new tile, a new vanity, better lighting and a new fan.

Can a tub be replaced with a walk-in shower?

Often. What decides it is the drain location, the floor framing and the dimensions of the room. We check those on site before the shower is drawn.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Montgomery County?

For most remodels, expect one or more. WSSC Water regulates plumbing in Montgomery County, and the Department of Permitting Services lists electrical work and interior alterations among projects that will likely need a County permit. The City of Rockville and Howard County run their own permit offices.

What can be done with a small bathroom?

Recessed niches, a wall-hung vanity or a pocket door can each give back floor space. Which of them fits depends on what is inside the walls, so it is decided after a look at the framing and pipes.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

It depends on the scope, on lead times for tile and shower glass, on permit review and on what turns up behind the walls. Once the project is defined, the schedule is confirmed in writing with the scope.
