More room inside the same walls.

Three projects that change how the house works without adding to it.

  • Combine the kitchen with the room beside it

    If a breakfast room, pantry or back hall sits next to the kitchen, taking it in can make space for an island or a table. If the wall between them carries the floor above, the opening needs a structural design first. If the kitchen stays the size it is, cabinets to the ceiling and a shallower pantry wall recover storage.

  • Add or rebuild a bathroom

    If the house has one bathroom per floor, a second can sometimes be made from a closet, the end of a hall or the corner of a bedroom. Where it goes depends on how the drain reaches the existing stack. If an existing bath is small, a shower in place of the tub and storage set into the wall give back floor space.

  • Finish the basement

    If the ceiling is low, the height is measured before anything else is planned, since ducts and beams take some of it. If the walls or floor show moisture, that is corrected before framing. If the laundry is already there, a finished laundry room and a bathroom can share its plumbing wall.

What is different about remodeling in Chevy Chase.

Several separate towns and villages, and the factors that shape a budget here.

  • County and WSSC. Montgomery County’s Department of Permitting Services issues the County building permit. Plumbing and gas go through WSSC Water.
  • Towns and villages. Chevy Chase includes several separate municipalities. The County’s list of municipalities shows which of them require their own approval and in what order. It places the Town of Chevy Chase, Section 3, Section 5, Martin’s Additions and Somerset among those where the County permit comes first.
  • Ask the municipality about your project. Each one decides what its own permit covers. The Town of Chevy Chase, for example, approves its building permit only after the County permit is issued.
  • Historic properties. Montgomery Planning’s map shows whether a property is designated. The County’s historic permit applies to exterior changes and is not required for interior work.

How do you assess the house before design starts?

We measure the rooms and note where the water, drain, gas and electrical lines run. We also look at the electrical panel and ask about earlier additions or remodels. If the house has plaster walls or older wiring and piping, the scope allows for what may be found once a wall is opened.

Can the kitchen take in the breakfast room or pantry beside it?

Yes, if the wall between them can be opened. What that wall carries is settled by an on-site structural assessment. A measured drawing then shows whether the larger room fits an island, a table or more counter along the wall.

Can a second bathroom fit inside the existing house?

It depends on finding space near the plumbing. A closet, the end of a hall or part of a bedroom can work if the new drain can reach the existing stack with the right slope. The room also needs ventilation to the outside and a waterproofed shower or tub.

Can a basement with a low ceiling be finished?

The ceiling height is measured first and checked against what the permit office requires for the rooms you want. If the height falls short, lowering the floor is structural work. Whether it is feasible in your house is decided by an on-site assessment and by the permit office.

What should we have ready for the consultation?

The address, the name of your town or village if you are in one, and photos of the rooms. A list of what is not working today helps, sorted into essential and optional. Bring any drawings of the house, a budget range and the timing you have in mind.

Do I need a permit from my town or village as well as the County?

It depends on the address and the work. The County lists the Chevy Chase municipalities that require their own approval in addition to the County permit, and the order differs from one to the next. Each municipality sets what its own permit covers, so the requirement for a given project is confirmed with its office.
