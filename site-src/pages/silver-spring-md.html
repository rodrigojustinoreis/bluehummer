Remodeling a Silver Spring home in phases

What to decide in each room, and what to prepare for the phase after it.

  • Kitchen remodeling

    If you cook every day, the plan starts with the path between sink, range and refrigerator, and with a hood ducted to the outside. If the family eats in the kitchen, a table and an island compete for the same floor, and a measured drawing settles which one fits. If the electrical panel is full, it is dealt with in this phase, because later rooms depend on it.

  • Bathroom remodeling

    If children use the bath, a tub with a shower stays practical. If you plan to stay in the house for the long run, a shower without a curb and wood blocking in the walls for future grab bars are built in now. If storage is short, a recessed cabinet and a niche in the shower use the depth of the wall.

  • Basement remodeling

    If only part of the basement is needed as living space, that part is finished and the rest stays as storage and mechanical room. If a bathroom may come later, its drain lines can be placed before the floor is finished. If the space will be an office, outlets, data wiring and a door are planned from the start.

Who reviews a remodel in Silver Spring

The County and WSSC Water review the work. An address in Takoma Park is also checked with the City.

  • County and WSSC

    Silver Spring is not on Montgomery County’s list of municipalities, so the Department of Permitting Services issues the building permit. Plumbing and gas go through WSSC Water.

  • Takoma Park

    The County’s Silver Spring regional office serves an area that includes all of the City of Takoma Park. Takoma Park is on the list of municipalities that require city approval, so an address there is checked with the City.

  • Historic properties

    Montgomery Planning’s map shows whether a property is identified as historic or lies in a historic district. The County’s historic permit applies to exterior changes and is not required for interior work.

  • Covenants and associations

    The County does not enforce private covenants. If the neighborhood has an association, its rules are checked with the association.

What do you check in the house before planning a remodel?

The measured rooms, the routes of the plumbing, gas and wiring, and the capacity of the electrical panel. In a basement we also look for moisture and measure the ceiling height. Structural questions are settled by an on-site assessment.

Should the kitchen or the bathroom come first?

Start with the room that causes the most trouble day to day. If both are planned, work that serves the whole house comes with the first one, such as electrical capacity. If one bathroom sits above the kitchen, its plumbing is looked at before the kitchen ceiling is closed.

Can a bathroom be made easier to use as we get older?

Yes, and it is simplest during a remodel. A shower without a curb, a wider door and lever handles are drawn into the plan. Wood blocking set inside the walls lets grab bars be added later without opening the tile.

Can we finish only part of the basement?

Yes. The finished part gets insulation, wiring, heating and cooling, and the rest stays as storage and mechanical space behind a door. Moisture is dealt with across the whole level first, since it does not stop at a partition.

What should we have ready for the consultation?

The address, photos of each room, and the full list of changes in order of priority. Add a budget range for the first phase and the timing you have in mind. If earlier work was done on the house, any drawings or permit records help.

Is Silver Spring a city with its own permit office?

Silver Spring does not appear on Montgomery County’s list of municipalities. The County’s Department of Permitting Services issues the building permit, and plumbing and gas go through WSSC Water. The City of Takoma Park is on that list and requires city approval.
