The homes we see here

1970s to 2000s colonials, ready for their second act.

Most of Olney was built between the early 1970s and the mid-2000s: center-hall and garage-front colonials, split-levels and the larger two-story-foyer homes of the 1990s. They are well built and generously sized, and many are reaching the age where the original builder kitchen and primary bath no longer match how the family lives.

  • Norbeck Grove, Hallowell & Lake Hallowell, Williamsburg Village, Olney Mill, Cherrywood, James Creek, Brooke Manor, Olney Oaks. Builder kitchens with oak cabinets, soffits and a wall to the family room; garden tubs that never get used; unfinished or half-finished walkout basements.
  • Brookeville, Brinklow, Ashton & Sandy Spring. Larger lots at the edge of the Agricultural Reserve, older farmhouses alongside 1990s–2010s customs, and a good share of homes on well and septic.
  • Laytonsville and the Route 108 corridor. Estate properties with room for a true scullery kitchen, a mudroom suite or a full lower-level apartment.

Permits & local specifics

What is different about remodeling in Olney.

  • Montgomery County DPS issues the permits. Olney, Ashton, Sandy Spring, Brinklow and Laytonsville are unincorporated; kitchen, bath and basement work that touches structure, plumbing or electrical is permitted and inspected by the Department of Permitting Services.
  • Brookeville adds historic review. The Town of Brookeville is a designated historic district, so exterior changes to homes inside town limits need approval in addition to the County permit. Interior-only work does not.
  • Well and septic matter. Adding a bedroom or bathroom to a home on septic triggers a review of septic capacity through DPS’s Well and Septic Section. We check this before the design phase, not after.
  • Basement egress. A lower-level bedroom needs a code-compliant egress window or door; on walkout lots this is often simple, on others it means a window well and a structural cut we plan carefully.

Yes. Brookeville, Brinklow, Ashton, Sandy Spring and Laytonsville are all part of our home territory and a few minutes’ drive from our base in Olney 20832.

Can you remove the wall between my kitchen and family room?

In most Olney colonials that wall is load-bearing, which means an engineered beam, temporary shoring and a structural permit through Montgomery County DPS. We do this regularly; the cost and the beam size are confirmed during design, not guessed.

My house is on septic. Can I still add a basement bathroom?

Usually, if the septic system has capacity for the home’s bedroom count. Adding a bedroom is the bigger trigger. We review the septic permit record with you before committing to a layout.

How long would a kitchen remodel in Olney take?

Plan on five to seven months from first conversation to finished kitchen: roughly two months of design and selections, two to three months of ordering and permit review, and six to ten weeks of construction.
