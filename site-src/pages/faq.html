Costs & budgets

What remodeling really costs around here.

The ranges below are planning ranges for established homes in Montgomery and Howard County, not quotes. Your proposal is priced from your drawings, your selections and the conditions we find in your home.

How much does a kitchen remodel cost in Montgomery County?

For a full kitchen remodel in an established Montgomery or Howard County home, most of our conversations land between roughly $85,000 and $175,000, and design-led projects with custom cabinetry, natural stone, new openings or relocated plumbing often run $175,000 to $300,000 and up. A cosmetic refresh that keeps the layout can be less. See the full breakdown on our kitchen remodeling page.

How much does a bathroom remodel cost?

A hall or guest bath typically plans between about $35,000 and $65,000. A primary bath with a curbless shower, freestanding tub, double vanity and heated floors usually runs $70,000 to $150,000 or more depending on size, tile and whether the layout changes. Details are on our bathroom remodeling page.

How much does it cost to finish a basement?

Finishing a lower level with a family room, a full bath, a bedroom with a code-compliant egress window and good lighting generally plans between about $90,000 and $200,000 in our area; adding a wet bar, home theater, gym or in-law suite moves it higher. Our basements page explains what drives the number.

Why do remodeling costs vary so much from house to house?

Three things move the number most: how much of the layout changes (moving plumbing, gas, structure), the finish level you choose (stock versus custom cabinetry, quartz versus quartzite or marble, standard versus large-format tile) and what we find once walls are open in a 1930s–1990s house: outdated wiring, undersized framing, old cast-iron drains, moisture. We price what we can see, allow for what we can reasonably expect and tell you where the uncertainty is.

Do you give free estimates?

The first conversation and a walkthrough to understand your goals, home and budget range cost nothing. For design-led projects, drawings and selections are usually handled as a defined design phase before construction pricing is finalized, because that work is what makes the final number reliable. We explain how it works for your project before you commit to anything.

Timelines & process

How long it takes and what happens when.

How long does a kitchen remodel take?

Plan on 4–8 weeks of design and selections, 6–10 weeks of ordering (custom cabinetry is the long pole), 2–6 weeks for permit review, and then roughly 6–10 weeks of construction for a full kitchen. From first call to finished kitchen, five to seven months is realistic for a design-led project.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

Construction on a hall bath usually takes 3–5 weeks; a primary bath with layout changes takes 5–8 weeks. Add design, selections, ordering and permitting ahead of that. Tile, plumbing fixtures and custom vanities drive the lead times.

How soon can my project start?

That depends on where you are in design and on our schedule. Tell us your preferred timeframe in the form; we will give you an honest picture rather than an optimistic one.

Do I need to have a finished design already?

No. Many clients come to us with a list of frustrations and a few saved images. We can work from that, from an architect’s drawings, or anywhere in between.

Can we live in the house during the remodel?

Usually, yes. For kitchens we set up a temporary kitchen zone; for a single bath we sequence the work so you always have a working bathroom. Whole-level or multi-room projects are planned room by room. How dust, floors and clean-up are handled is agreed with you before work starts.

Permits, licensing & service area

The practical questions.

Will permits be needed for my project?

Almost always for kitchens, baths and basements that touch plumbing, electrical, gas, structure or add habitable space. We prepare and file the application. The office depends on your address: Montgomery County Department of Permitting Services for most of our area, the City of Rockville inside city limits, and Howard County DILP for Clarksville, Highland, Fulton, Ellicott City and the rest of the Howard corridor. Some towns, including Chevy Chase, Kensington, Garrett Park and Brookeville, add their own review for exterior or historic work.

Is Blue Hammer licensed and insured?

Maryland requires home improvement contractors to hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission (MHIC) license. Before signing with anyone, including us, verify the license number on the MHIC website and ask for current insurance certificates. Our license number is published in the footer of this site as soon as it is confirmed for display.
