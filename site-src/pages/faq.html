Remodeling questions and answers.

What homeowners ask before a kitchen, bathroom or basement remodel in Maryland: what a budget is made of, what sets the schedule, which office issues the permit and how to check a contractor.

What makes up a remodeling budget

A budget is the sum of a few decisions. These answers name them; the price for a project comes from its drawings and selections.

What decides the cost of a kitchen remodel?

How much of the layout changes is the first factor: a kitchen that keeps its sink, range and walls in place needs far less plumbing, gas and electrical work than one that moves them. After that come the cabinet line, the counter material, the appliances, and the wiring and pipes the house already has. The kitchen remodeling page compares the scopes side by side.

What decides the cost of a bathroom remodel?

Whether the drains and supply lines stay where they are. Beyond that, the amount of tile, the shower enclosure, the vanity and the condition of the old plumbing make up most of the difference between one bathroom and another. The bathroom remodeling page sets out the scopes.

What decides the cost of finishing a basement?

What the space has to do. An open family room is the simplest scope. A bathroom or a wet bar adds plumbing below the main floor, enclosed rooms add doors, wiring and heating and cooling changes, and any moisture problem is corrected first. See the basements and interiors page.

Why do two similar projects end up with different budgets?

Three things account for most of it: how much of the layout changes, the products chosen (stock or made-to-order cabinetry, the counter material, the size and type of tile) and the condition of the house, part of which can only be confirmed once walls are open. A written scope lists what is included, what is an allowance and how a hidden condition is handled.

When do I get a price for my project?

After the scope is defined. The first conversation and the walkthrough cover your goals, the house and your budget range. Drawings and selections follow, and the written proposal is priced from them, line by line.

How a schedule is set

How long does a remodel take?

Four things set it: the design decisions still open, how long the selected products take to arrive, the permit review for your address and the amount of construction. A project that keeps the layout and uses stock products is the shortest case. Once the scope is defined, the schedule is confirmed in writing with it.

How soon can my project start?

A start date depends on how far along the design is, on permit approval where a permit is needed and on delivery of the products ordered for the job. Tell us your preferred timing in the form and we will say what it depends on for your project.

Do I need to have a finished design already?

No. A list of what is not working and a few saved photos are enough to start. Drawings from an architect or designer work too.

Can we live in the house during the remodel?

It depends on the scope, and it is decided during planning. If the kitchen is being remodeled, the plan needs to cover where you will cook and wash up in the meantime. If the only bathroom in the house is being remodeled, the plan needs to cover the time it is out of use, or whether being away for that stretch makes more sense. How dust, floors and access are handled is agreed with you before work starts.

What if something unexpected turns up behind a wall?

It is described in writing with its cost and its effect on the schedule, and you approve it before that work is done. The same applies to any change you ask for mid-project.

Permits, credentials and service area

Will permits be needed for my project?

It depends on the work and on the address. In Montgomery County, the Department of Permitting Services lists electrical work and interior alterations among home improvements that will likely need a permit, and cabinet replacement, floor coverings and painting among those that most likely will not. Plumbing and gas go through WSSC Water. The City of Rockville issues its own permits inside city limits, and Howard County has its own office, the Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits. Which permits your project needs, and who applies for each, is confirmed in writing before work is accepted.

Does my town require its own approval?

Some do. The County publishes a list of municipalities that require their own approval in addition to the County permit, and the order differs from one to the next. Historic status is checked property by property. The service areas page links to the page for each town.

How do I check a remodeling contractor’s credentials in Maryland?

The Maryland Home Improvement Commission, part of the Maryland Department of Labor, licenses and regulates home improvement contractors and salespersons. Its public license search finds a contractor by personal name, trade name, city or ZIP code, or license number. Before you sign a home improvement contract with any company, ask the person responsible for it for the license number and the name it is registered under, look both up yourself, and ask for a current certificate of insurance. Washington, DC and Virginia have their own licensing offices.
