More from the rooms you already have.

A remodel does not have to add square footage. Each plan starts from where the walls and pipes are now, and a few situations change the options.

  • Kitchen remodeling

    If the kitchen sits on an outside wall, the sink can stay under the window and the range hood has a short path outdoors. If it is closed off from the dining room, that wall can be widened or removed once an on-site assessment shows whether it carries load. If floor space is tight, storage comes from drawer bases, a pantry cabinet and cabinets run to the ceiling.

  • Bathroom remodeling

    If the hall bath has the only tub in the house, decide whether to keep one before choosing a walk-in shower. If the room has no window, the exhaust fan and its duct route are planned first. If storage is short, a vanity with drawers and a recessed niche add it without taking floor space.

  • Basement finishing

    If the ceiling is low or crossed by ducts and beams, the rooms are drawn around them. If water has ever shown on the floor or walls, that is dealt with before any finish goes in. If you want a bathroom, the position of the drain line decides where it can go.

Bethesda permits, in brief.

What your project needs is confirmed in writing before work is accepted.

  • County and WSSC. Bethesda is not one of Montgomery County’s municipalities, so the Department of Permitting Services issues the building permit. It requires one before a house is renovated, other than a repair. Plumbing and gas go through WSSC Water.
  • Historic properties. If the house is designated historic, as in the Greenwich Forest district, the County’s Historic Area Work Permit applies to exterior changes and is not required for interior work. Montgomery Planning’s map shows the status of each property.
  • Glen Echo. The Town is on the County’s list of municipalities whose approval is needed in addition to the County permit.

How do you assess a house before designing a remodel?

We walk the room with you and measure it. We note where the water, gas and electrical lines run, because they set how far the layout can move. Anything structural is settled by an on-site assessment, never from a photo or a floor plan.

Can a kitchen work better without adding on to the house?

Yes, when the existing walls allow it. Options include moving a doorway, widening the opening to the dining room, or replacing a peninsula with an island. Whether a wall can be opened depends on whether it carries load, which is checked on site.

Should we replace the bathtub with a walk-in shower?

If another bathroom in the house keeps a tub, a shower alone is a simple choice. If this is the only tub, think about who uses it now and who will later. Either way, the waterproofing behind the tile matters more than the fixture.

Can a basement with a low ceiling be finished?

It depends on the measured height and on what crosses the ceiling. Ducts and pipes can sometimes be rerouted or boxed in. The minimum height for a finished room comes from the building code and is confirmed with the permit office. Moisture is checked first in every case.

What should we have ready for the consultation?

The project address or ZIP code, a few photos of the room, and a wish list sorted into essential and optional. A budget range and the timing you have in mind help too. If an architect or designer has drawn plans, bring them.

Who issues the permit for a remodel in Bethesda, MD?

Montgomery County’s Department of Permitting Services issues the building permit, and plumbing and gas go through WSSC Water. Bethesda is not one of the County’s municipalities, so there is no city permit. A designated historic property needs a separate County permit for exterior changes only.
